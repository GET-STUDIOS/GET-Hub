📖 GET Survival Field Manual

Practical survival knowledge, emergency procedures, bushcraft and family preparedness.

📚 Quick Index

💧Water Purification & Storage+
  • Water priority: Safe drinking water should be treated as an immediate priority during prolonged disruptions.
  • Boiling: Bring clear water to a rolling boil for at least 1 minute (3+ minutes at high altitudes). Allow it to cool in a clean covered container.
  • Filtration: Use a hollow-fiber membrane or ceramic water filter whenever possible. Know what pore size and contaminants the filter removes.
  • Chemical treatment: Use chlorine dioxide or household bleach (unscented 5-6%) when boiling is impossible. Follow exact dosage and contact times.
  • Storage: Store at least 3 to 4 liters per person per day in food-safe BPA-free containers. Keep out of direct sunlight and off concrete floors.
  • Redundancy: Maintain at least two separate independent purification methods.
🔥Fire Making & Ignition Mastery+
  • Tinder: Gather ultra-dry fibrous materials like birch bark, fatwood shavings, dry grass, or cotton balls soaked in petroleum jelly.
  • Kindling: Collect pencil-thick dry twigs and sticks. Never lay wood directly on wet ground; build a raised platform.
  • Fuel Logs: Hardwoods burn slower and hotter than softwoods. Sort wood by size before lighting.
  • Ignition Redundancy: Carry stormproof matches, a ferrocerium rod (ferro rod), and a reliable butane lighter in waterproof containers.
  • Oxygen Flow: Arrange fuel in a teepee or log-cabin structure to ensure adequate airflow underneath the flame.
  • Extinguishing: Drown with water, stir embers, and feel the ash with the back of your hand to ensure 0% heat before departure.
⛺Emergency Shelter & Insulation+
  • Location Safety: Avoid flash-flood riverbeds, widow-makers (dead leaning branches), avalanche zones, and exposed ridge tops.
  • Ground Insulation: Conduction steals body heat 20x faster than air. Layer pine boughs, leaves, or sleeping pads underneath your body.
  • Tarp Configuration: Rig an A-frame or lean-to tarp setup using paracord anchored to sturdy trees or trekking poles.
  • Windbreak: Position your shelter opening perpendicular to prevailing winds or natural terrain barriers.
  • Thermal Management: Keep sleeping gear bone dry. Change out of sweaty clothing before resting.
🧭Navigation & Land Orienteering+
  • Topographic Literacy: Understand contour lines—close lines indicate steep slopes; wide lines indicate flat terrain.
  • Compass Discipline: Always account for magnetic declination in your geographic region. Keep compass away from metal tools and electronics.
  • Handrails & Catching Features: Use linear features (rivers, roads, ridges) as handrails and prominent features as backstops to avoid overshooting.
  • Dead Reckoning: Track your pacing (count double-steps per 100 meters) and elapsed travel time.
  • Backup Tech: Keep offline vector maps downloaded on mobile devices with external power banks.
🩹Trauma & Advanced First Aid+
  • MARCH PAWS Protocol: Massive Hemorrhage, Airway, Respiration, Circulation, Hypothermia/Head, Pain management, Administration, Wounds, Splinting.
  • Tourniquet Application: Apply high and tight on extremities for severe arterial bleeding. Never loosen a properly placed tourniquet in the field.
  • Wound Packing: Pack deep junctional wounds tightly with gauze and hold direct pressure for a minimum of 3 minutes.
  • Burn Care: Cool burns with cool (not freezing) water for 10-20 minutes. Do not rupture blisters; cover loosely with sterile non-adherent dressings.
  • Shock Treatment: Keep the casualty warm, lying flat with feet slightly elevated unless head or chest trauma is suspected.
🍲Emergency Nutrition & Preservation+
  • FIFO Rotation: First In, First Out. Regularly rotate stock items to prevent spoilage during long-term storage.
  • Caloric Density: Prioritize calorie-dense, shelf-stable foods (rice, oats, legumes, peanut butter, honey, canned meats).
  • Off-Grid Cooking: Maintain propane camping stoves, multi-fuel burners, or a Dutch oven setup for outdoor cooking.
  • Pest Prevention: Store dry grains in airtight food-grade buckets with oxygen absorbers and gamma seal lids.
  • Foraging Warning: Never consume wild plants, mushrooms, or berries unless 100% positively identified by an expert.
🧼Hygiene & Waste Management+
  • Handwashing: Wash hands thoroughly with soap for 20 seconds before food prep and after latrine use. Use hand sanitizer (>60% alcohol) when water is scarce.
  • Cat-Holes: Dig emergency latrines at least 60 meters (200 feet) away from natural water sources, trails, and camp areas, at least 15-20 cm deep.
  • Graywater Disposal: Scatter dishwater and washing water well away from natural water bodies using biodegradable soap.
  • Vector Control: Keep garbage sealed in heavy-duty bags to prevent attracting rodents, flies, and predators.
⚡Grid-Down Power & Battery Discipline+
  • Load Shedding: Turn off high-draw appliances immediately during grid failures. Prioritize medical equipment, lighting, and radios.
  • Solar Charging: Position portable solar panels at optimal angles perpendicular to midday sun. Keep panels clean and free of shade.
  • Battery Storage: Store LiFePO4 and power banks at 50-60% charge in climate-controlled, dry environments.
  • Rechargeable Rotation: Label rechargeable AA/AAA batteries so depleted cells are never mixed with fresh ones.
📻Radio Operations & Protocols+
  • Phonetic Alphabet: Use Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel... for spelling critical call signs and locations over voice radio.
  • Schedule Windows: Establish predetermined check-in windows (e.g., every 4 hours on the hour) to conserve radio battery life.
  • Signal Reports: Use Readability-Strength-Tone (RST) system. Example: "5 by 9" indicates perfect audio clarity and maximum signal strength.
  • Squelch Adjustment: Adjust radio squelch just until static background hiss disappears to catch weak distant transmissions.
🚗Vehicle Hardening & Recovery+
  • Fuel Discipline: Never let your vehicle tank drop below 1/2 full during unstable periods.
  • Self-Recovery Gear: Carry kinetic tow straps, heavy-duty shackles, traction boards, a folding shovel, and tire repair plugs.
  • Bug-Out Maintenance: Check oil, coolant, brake fluid, windshield washer fluid, and tire pressures weekly.
  • Glass Breaker / Seatbelt Cutter: Mount an emergency egress tool securely within arm's reach of the driver seat.
🎒Evacuation & Bug-Out Protocols+
  • The 72-Hour Rule: Every bug-out bag must sustain an individual independently for a minimum of 3 days without resupply.
  • Primary vs Alternate Routes: Always plan two distinct evacuation routes off your grid square to bypass potential roadblocks.
  • Rally Points: Establish an immediate neighborhood rally point and an off-site regional rally point.
  • Roll-Call Verification: Confirm every GET member's status before initiating movement.
🌪️General Disaster Protocols+
  • Earthquake: Drop to hands and knees, Cover your head/neck under sturdy furniture, and Hold On until shaking stops.
  • Flash Flood: Move immediately to higher ground. Turn Around, Don't Drown—just 15 cm of moving water can sweep adults off their feet.
  • Severe Thunderstorm: Seek rigid shelter indoors immediately. Unplug sensitive electronics. Avoid tall isolated trees and metal fences.
  • Tsunami: If near the coast and you feel a strong earthquake lasting 20+ seconds, evacuate immediately to high ground without waiting for sirens.
🔥Wildfire Defense & Survival+
  • Defensible Space: Clear flammable brush, dry leaves, and wood piles at least 10 meters away from structures.
  • Ember Defense: Close all windows, doors, and attic vents to prevent wind-driven embers from igniting interiors.
  • Evacuate Early: Never wait for flames to become visible before leaving. Smoke inhalation and road gridlock are primary hazards.
  • Protective Clothing: Wear 100% cotton or wool garments, sturdy boots, and an N95 mask/respirator to filter toxic smoke particulates.
🌊Flood Preparedness & Post-Flood+
  • Utility Shutdown: Turn off electricity at the main breaker and gas valves if instructed or if waters rise inside the home.
  • Contamination Warning: Assume all floodwater is contaminated with sewage, chemicals, and biological pathogens. Wear waterproof boots and gloves.
  • Mold Prevention: Dry out water-damaged structures within 24-48 hours using cross-ventilation and dehumidifiers.
  • Bridge Safety: Never cross washed-out bridges or flooded roads, as roadbeds may have eroded underneath.
🌪️Tornado & Hurricane Preparedness+
  • Tornado Safe Room: Move to a basement, cellar, or interior windowless room on the lowest floor (bathroom or hallway). Cover head with mattress.
  • Hurricane Shutters: Install storm shutters or plywood over glass windows before high winds arrive. Secure loose outdoor patio furniture.
  • Eye of the Storm: During a hurricane, remember the calm eye is temporary; violent winds will resume from the opposite direction.
☀️Extreme Heat & Heatstroke Prevention+
  • Hydration Discipline: Drink water before feeling thirsty (approx. 500ml-1L per hour in extreme exertion). Include electrolyte replacements.
  • Heat Exhaustion Symptoms: Heavy sweating, dizziness, nausea, headache, clammy skin. Move to shade and cool down immediately.
  • Heatstroke (Medical Emergency): High body temperature (>39°C), confusion, loss of consciousness, dry hot skin (no sweating). Immerse in cool water or apply ice packs immediately.
❄️Cold Weather & Hypothermia Survival+
  • The CBGT Rule: Cotton Kills (retains moisture and chills body). Wear moisture-wicking synthetics or wool baselayers, insulating midlayers, and windproof outer shell.
  • Hypothermia Stages: Shivering (mild), confusion/slurred speech (moderate), unresponsiveness/loss of shivering (severe). Handle casualties extremely gently.
  • Frostbite Care: Never rub frostbitten skin. Warm gradually in warm water (37-39°C) only if there is zero risk of refreezing.
  • Tent Ventilation: When using heaters inside winter tents, always maintain cross-ventilation to prevent lethal carbon monoxide poisoning.
☢️Nuclear & Radiological Emergency Protocol+
  • Immediate Shelter: Get Inside, Stay Inside, Stay Tuned. Move to the center of a basement or multi-story concrete building immediately.
  • Fallout Shielding: Dense materials like concrete, thick earth, and brick provide optimal radiation attenuation. Stay sheltered for at least 24-48 hours.
  • Decontamination: Remove outer layers of contaminated clothing and shoes at the entryway. Shower thoroughly with soap and lukewarm water.
  • Air Sealing: Turn off HVAC systems and seal windows/doors with plastic sheeting and duct tape if instructed by civil defense authorities.
🏠Home Safe Room Establishment+
  • Room Selection: Choose an internal room with no exterior walls or windows, reinforced ceiling if possible.
  • Dedicated Cache: Stock water reserves, emergency rations, battery lanterns, hand-crank radio, first aid kit, and portable chemical toilet.
  • Communication Hub: Keep backup PMR/walkie-talkies and printed contact sheets inside the safe room.
🕯️Extended Blackout Protocol+
  • First 10 Minutes: Verify whether the outage is localized (check household breaker panel) or grid-wide. Conserve phone batteries.
  • Refrigerator Management: Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed. A closed freezer maintains safe temperatures for up to 48 hours.
  • Water Preservation: Fill bathtubs and spare containers with tap water immediately upon notice of grid/water infrastructure failure.
🪢Bushcraft & Knot Tying+
  • Square Knot: Joining two ropes of equal diameter securely.
  • Bowline: The "King of Knots"—creates a secure, non-slip loop under heavy loads that is easy to untie afterwards.
  • Taut-Line Hitch: Adjustable loop for tensioning tent guy lines and tarps.
  • Clove Hitch: Fast starting and ending hitch for securing ropes to posts or spars.
🐾Wildlife & Dangerous Encounters+
  • Bear Encounter: Do not run. Stand tall, speak calmly, and back away slowly. If attacked by a grizzly/brown bear, play dead; if black bear, fight back aggressively.
  • Wild Boar / Large Mammals: Give wide berth, especially if mothers are accompanied by young. Climb a sturdy tree if charged.
  • Snakebites: Keep calm, immobilize the affected limb below heart level, remove restrictive jewelry, and seek urgent medical evacuation. Never cut or suck venom.
  • Tick Removal: Use fine-tipped tweezers to grasp the tick as close to the skin as possible. Pull straight up firmly and disinfect the bite area.
🗺️Route Planning & Rally Points+
  • Primary Evacuation Vector: Highway/road corridor mapped out to regional safe zones.
  • Secondary Off-Road Route: Secondary forestry tracks or foot trails bypassing chokepoints.
  • Rally Point Alpha: Local neighborhood assembly point (within 1 km).
  • Rally Point Bravo: Regional high-ground rendezvous point (outside urban perimeter).
📦Long-Term Grid Disruption Survival+
  • Sustained Water Security: Establish multi-tier collection (rainwater harvesting, gravity filters, boiling stations).
  • Food Security: Supplement stored rations with microgreens, sprouted grains, or community bartering networks.
  • Mental Resilience: Maintain daily routines, assigned community chores, physical exercise, and evening debriefs to combat stress.
🧰Improvised Repairs & Field Craft+
  • Duct Tape & Paracord: The universal fix-all for gear tears, splints, tool handles, and temporary shelter lashings.
  • Zip Ties & Wire: Essential for securing loose vehicle bumpers, hoses, and fencing repairs.
  • Epoxy / Super Glue: Fast structural bonding for cracked tool grips, cookware handles, and eye-wear frames.
🧠Mindset & Team Psychology+
  • STOP Formula: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan. Never panic or act rashly under sudden stress.
  • Leadership Roles: Delegate clear responsibilities (Medics, Communicators, Quartermasters, Security) to maintain order.
  • Calm Communication: Keep discussions objective, reassuring, and morale-focused, especially around younger team members.
🪪Vital Documents & Secure Records+
  • Physical Waterproof Copies: Passports, IDs, medical history cards, insurance policies, and property deeds stored in a waterproof pouch.
  • Encrypted Digital Backups: USB flash drives and secure cloud storage containing scanned copies.
  • Emergency Contact Ledger: Printed list of family numbers, blood types, allergies, and local emergency dispatch numbers.
🧯Structural Fire Safety & Suppression+
  • PASS Method (Extinguishers): Pull pin, Aim low at the base of fire, Squeeze handle, Sweep side to side.
  • Smoke Evacuation: Crawl low under smoke where cleaner, cooler air remains near the floor.
  • Door Check: Feel closed doors with the back of your hand before opening. If hot, use an alternate escape route.
  • Never Re-Enter: Once evacuated from a burning structure, never re-enter for any reason.
📋Universal Survival Priorities+
1 Account for every GET team member.
2 Identify and mitigate immediate life threats.
3 Establish protection from extreme weather (Shelter).
4 Secure clean drinking water (Hydration).
5 Stabilize injuries and administer First Aid.
6 Establish communication links & monitor local feeds.
7 Assess whether to shelter in place or evacuate.
8 Conserve energy, battery reserves, and food.

GET Field Manual: This manual is a preparedness reference and does not replace professional medical, emergency or technical training. Follow official local emergency instructions whenever available.
