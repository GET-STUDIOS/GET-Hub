📖 GET Survival Field Manual

Practical survival knowledge, emergency procedures, bushcraft and family preparedness.

📚 Quick Index

💧 Water Purification & Storage +
  • Water priority: Safe drinking water should be treated as an immediate priority during prolonged disruptions.
  • Boiling: Bring clear water to a rolling boil. Allow it to cool in a clean covered container.
  • Filtration: Use a purpose-built water filter whenever possible. Know what the filter is designed to remove.
  • Chemical treatment: Follow the manufacturer's dosage and contact time exactly.
  • Storage: Use clean food-safe containers and keep stored water away from sunlight and contamination.
  • Redundancy: Maintain more than one purification method.
🔥Fire Making +
  • Tinder: Prepare several dry, easily ignited materials before attempting ignition.
  • Kindling: Collect progressively larger dry material before lighting the fire.
  • Fuel: Use suitable untreated dry wood.
  • Ignition: Carry at least two independent ignition systems.
  • Airflow: Build the fire so oxygen can reach the flame.
  • Safety: Clear combustible material around the fire and extinguish it completely before leaving.
⛺Emergency Shelter & Insulation +
  • Location: Avoid flood channels, dead trees, unstable slopes and rockfall areas.
  • Weather: Position the shelter to reduce wind and rain exposure.
  • Ground insulation: Insulate underneath the body to reduce heat loss.
  • Tarps: A tarp can provide fast overhead and wind protection.
  • Dryness: Keep sleeping equipment dry whenever possible.
🧭Navigation & Land Navigation +
  • Map first: Learn to read local maps and identify major terrain features.
  • Compass: Maintain a physical compass as a backup to electronic navigation.
  • Landmarks: Use roads, ridges, streams, buildings and terrain features to confirm position.
  • Bearings: Record important bearings before beginning a route.
  • Pacing: Learn your approximate distance-per-100-paces on familiar terrain.
  • GPS: Treat GPS as a useful tool, not the only navigation method.
🩹First Aid & Medical Basics +
  • Scene safety: Do not become another casualty.
  • Immediate threats: Focus first on major bleeding, breathing problems and responsiveness.
  • Bleeding: Apply firm direct pressure and use appropriate dressings.
  • Burns: Cool thermal burns with cool running water when appropriate.
  • Injuries: Avoid unnecessary movement of suspected serious injuries.
  • Medical kit: Maintain dressings, gloves, bandages, antiseptic supplies and required personal medication.
🍲Food, Cooking & Storage +
  • Rotation: Use older food first and replace it with newer stock.
  • Calories: Prioritize foods the household actually eats.
  • Cooking: Maintain at least one method that works without the electrical grid.
  • Fuel: Keep track of cooking fuel and its storage condition.
  • Hygiene: Prevent cross-contamination during food preparation.
🧼Sanitation & Hygiene +
  • Hand hygiene: Maintain soap, water and/or suitable hand sanitizer.
  • Waste: Keep human waste away from living and food areas.
  • Toilet backup: Have an emergency sanitation method ready before the plumbing fails.
  • Cleaning: Keep separate water for drinking and cleaning where practical.
  • Supplies: Store toilet paper, wipes, soap, bags and personal hygiene products.
⚡Power Outages & Energy Management +
  • Reduce load: Turn off non-essential equipment.
  • Lighting: Prefer LED lanterns and headlamps.
  • Charging: Rotate power banks and rechargeable batteries.
  • Solar: Prioritize communications, lighting and critical equipment.
  • Battery discipline: Keep charged and depleted batteries clearly organized.
📻Emergency Communications +
  • Team channels: Keep your agreed lawful radio channels recorded offline.
  • Check-ins: Use scheduled communications to conserve batteries.
  • Message format: Keep emergency messages short and clear.
  • Paper backup: Record important telephone numbers and addresses offline.
  • Rally points: Everyone should know where to meet if communications fail.
🚗Vehicle Emergency Preparedness +
  • Fuel: Avoid operating with an unnecessarily low fuel level during emergencies.
  • Recovery: Carry appropriate recovery and tire equipment.
  • Visibility: Keep legally required warning equipment accessible.
  • Emergency supplies: Water, food, blankets, first aid, lighting and charging equipment.
  • Maintenance: Check tires, fluids, battery, lights and wipers.
🎒Evacuation & Bug-Out +
  • Go early: Do not wait for evacuation routes to become congested when official guidance indicates departure.
  • Priority items: People, medication, identification, communications, water and critical documents.
  • Primary route: Maintain a preferred evacuation route.
  • Alternate route: Maintain at least one alternative.
  • Rally points: Establish family meeting points before emergencies occur.
  • Roll call: Confirm all GET members before and after movement.
🌪️General Disaster Protocols +
  • Earthquake: Drop, Cover and Hold On.
  • Flood: Move to higher ground and avoid flowing water.
  • Storm: Shelter indoors and monitor official warnings.
  • Wildfire: Prepare evacuation early.
  • Tsunami: Move inland and to higher ground after official warnings or strong/long coastal earthquakes.
  • Heat: Reduce exertion and maintain hydration.
🔥Wildfire Survival +
  • Prepare: Keep evacuation gear and documents ready.
  • Monitor: Follow official wildfire warnings.
  • Smoke: Reduce smoke entering the home where practical.
  • Vehicle: Keep access routes clear.
  • Evacuate: Leave when instructed rather than waiting for flames to approach.
🌊Flood Preparedness +
  • High ground: Know where elevated safe areas are located.
  • Electricity: Avoid wet or submerged electrical equipment.
  • Vehicles: Never assume a flooded road is safe.
  • Documents: Store critical documents in waterproof protection.
  • Water: Treat floodwater as contaminated.
🌪️Tornado & Severe Storms +
  • Shelter: Use a small interior room away from windows when tornado conditions threaten.
  • Protection: Protect your head and neck.
  • Outside: Avoid exposed areas and do not use bridges as shelters.
  • After the storm: Watch for broken glass, structural damage, gas leaks and electrical hazards.
☀️Extreme Heat +
  • Hydration: Drink regularly and monitor for signs of dehydration.
  • Shade: Reduce direct sun exposure.
  • Timing: Perform heavy work during cooler periods.
  • Warning signs: Confusion, fainting and severe overheating require urgent medical attention.
❄️Cold Weather Survival +
  • Layers: Dress in multiple layers.
  • Dryness: Keep clothing and bedding dry.
  • Insulation: Block wind and insulate from the ground.
  • Heating: Maintain safe heating methods and adequate fuel.
  • Carbon monoxide: Never use combustion appliances unsafely indoors.
☢️Nuclear / Radiological Emergency +
  • Get inside: Move indoors quickly after a radiological emergency warning.
  • Shielding: Put as much dense material between you and the outside as practical.
  • Stay inside: Follow official emergency information.
  • Decontamination: Remove contaminated outer clothing carefully and wash exposed skin.
  • Supplies: Use protected food and water.
  • Information: Follow official broadcasts rather than rumors.
🏠Home Safe Room +
  • Location: Prefer an interior room with limited windows where appropriate.
  • Supplies: Water, lighting, radio, first aid, sanitation and communications equipment.
  • Documents: Protect critical records.
  • Power: Keep charged lights and power banks ready.
  • Family knowledge: Every team member should know the safe-room procedure.
🕯️Blackout Procedures +
  • First minutes: Confirm the outage and switch off unnecessary equipment.
  • Lighting: Gather flashlights and lanterns immediately.
  • Food: Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed.
  • Water: Store suitable water while normal supply remains available when appropriate.
  • Communication: Establish contact with team members.
🪢Bushcraft & Field Skills +
  • Knots: Learn the square knot, bowline, clove hitch and basic tensioning methods.
  • Tools: Maintain and safely use knives, saws and axes.
  • Tinder: Learn how to identify suitable natural fire-starting materials.
  • Improvisation: Understand how cordage, containers and cloth can serve multiple purposes.
  • Practice: Train skills before an emergency occurs.
🐾Wildlife & Outdoor Safety +
  • Distance: Do not approach wildlife.
  • Food: Store food securely.
  • Snakes: Watch where hands and feet are placed around rocks and vegetation.
  • Ticks: Check yourself after outdoor activity.
  • Insects: Be alert for nests and stinging insects.
🗺️Route Planning & Rally Points +
  • Primary route: Establish the preferred route out of the area.
  • Alternative: Identify another route if the primary route is blocked.
  • Rally Point 1: Choose a nearby location accessible without a vehicle.
  • Rally Point 2: Choose a second location outside the immediate danger area.
  • Offline plan: Keep the routes documented on paper.
📦Long-Term Disruption +
  • Water: Maintain storage, treatment and multiple sources.
  • Food: Rotate stored food and maintain reliable cooking capability.
  • Energy: Reduce dependence on a single energy source.
  • Sanitation: Maintain sustainable hygiene and waste systems.
  • Repair: Keep tools, spare parts and repair materials.
  • Routine: Maintain sleep, meals and family routines.
🧰Improvised Repairs & Resourcefulness +
  • Repair kit: Duct tape, cordage, zip ties, wire, glue and sewing materials are useful multipurpose supplies.
  • Reuse: Repurpose containers and household materials where appropriate.
  • Tools: Maintain basic hand tools.
  • Temporary repairs: Record what still requires a permanent repair.
  • Spare parts: Keep replacements for commonly used equipment.
🧠Mindset, Stress & Team Discipline +
  • Pause: Stop and assess before acting when time permits.
  • Priorities: People, immediate hazards, shelter, water, medical needs and communication.
  • Information: Distinguish verified information from rumors.
  • Roles: Assign clear responsibilities during emergencies.
  • Check-ins: Use regular GET roll-calls.
  • Family: Keep communication calm and age-appropriate.
🪪Important Documents & Emergency Records +
  • Copies: Keep copies of important identification and family records.
  • Digital backup: Maintain secure digital copies.
  • Emergency contacts: Keep numbers and addresses on paper.
  • Medical information: Keep essential medical information accessible during emergencies.
  • Protection: Store physical copies in waterproof protection.
🧯Home Fire Safety +
  • Extinguishers: Maintain appropriate extinguishers and know where they are.
  • Escape: Know your available exits.
  • Smoke: Stay low in smoke-filled areas and evacuate.
  • Gas: Know how to safely isolate gas supplies when necessary.
  • Electrical: Avoid overloaded circuits and damaged equipment.
  • Re-entry: Never re-enter a burning or structurally unsafe building.
📋Emergency Priorities +
1 Account for every GET team member.
2 Identify immediate hazards.
3 Reach or establish safe shelter.
4 Secure safe drinking water.
5 Address urgent medical needs.
6 Establish communications.
7 Decide whether to shelter or evacuate.
8 Conserve food, water, power and batteries.

GET Field Manual: This manual is a preparedness reference and does not replace professional medical, emergency or technical training. Follow official local emergency instructions whenever available.
