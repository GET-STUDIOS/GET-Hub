📖 GET Survival & Emergency Field Manual

Comprehensive step-by-step practical guides for off-grid survival, emergency response, and bushcraft.

💧1. Water Procurement, Filtration & Purification+

Core Rule of 3s: Humans can survive only 3 days without potable water. Never drink raw surface water without treatment.

Step-by-Step Water Treatment Protocol:

  1. Step 1: Water Sourcing: Prioritize fast-flowing streams over stagnant ponds. Collect rainwater using clean tarps or extract morning dew using cotton clothes wrapped around legs while walking through tall grass.
  2. Step 2: Pre-Filtration (Sediment Removal): Pass murky water through a bandana, coffee filter, or a layered DIY tripod filter (grass, sand, crushed charcoal, gravel) to remove suspended dirt and large particulates.
  3. Step 3: Biological Sterilization (Choose 1 method):
    • Rolling Boil: Heat water to a vigorous rolling boil for 1 full minute (3 minutes at elevations above 2,000m). Kills 100% of viruses, bacteria, and protozoa.
    • Chemical Purification: Add 2 drops of unscented household bleach (5-6% sodium hypochlorite) per 1 Liter of clear water. Stir and let sit for 30 minutes in a sealed container before drinking.
    • Hollow-Fiber Membrane Filter: Push water through a 0.1-micron portable water filter (e.g. Sawyer Mini, LifeStraw) to clear bacteria and cysts.
  4. Step 4: Safe Storage & Maintenance: Store purified water in food-grade, opaque BPA-free containers away from direct sunlight. Rotate home water reserves every 6 months.
💡 Pro Tip: SODIS (Solar Water Disinfection) Method — Fill clear PET plastic bottles with clear water and expose to direct sunlight for 6 hours. UV rays neutralize pathogens when boiling fuel is unavailable.
🔥2. Fire Crafts & Ignition Mastery+

Fire provides heat, water purification, cooking capability, wild animal protection, and rescue signaling.

Step-by-Step Fire Building Process:

  1. Step 1: Prepare Ground & Firebed: Clear a 1-meter radius down to bare mineral soil. Build a base of dry logs or bark to insulate the fire from cold or wet ground.
  2. Step 2: Gather Materials in 3 Sizes:
    • Tinder: Dryer lint, vaseline-soaked cotton balls, shredded birch bark, or fine feather sticks (fist-sized bundle).
    • Kindling: Small dry twigs ranging from matchstick to pencil thickness (armful quantity).
    • Fuel Wood: Hardwood logs ranging from wrist to thigh thickness.
  3. Step 3: Construct the Structure: Build a classic Tepee or Log Cabin structure over your tinder bundle, leaving a windward opening for oxygen flow.
  4. Step 4: Ignition Step-by-Step: Hold a ferrocerium rod at a 45-degree angle right against the tinder. Scrape the spine of your knife firmly down the rod to shower 3000°C sparks into the tinder. Gently blow on the ember until it bursts into flame.
  5. Step 5: Fire Extinction (Cold Out): Douse with water, stir ashes with a stick, douse again. Touch embers with the back of your hand to ensure it is cold to the touch before leaving.
⛺3. Emergency Shelter Construction+

Hypothermia is the #1 cause of death in wilderness survival. A shelter traps body heat and protects against rain and wind.

Step-by-Step Debris Hut (Insulated Survival Shelter):

  1. Step 1: Site Selection: Find flat ground free of overhead hazards ("widowmaker" dead branches, bee hives, high-water flash flood channels).
  2. Step 2: Main Ridgepole Placement: Secure a sturdy branch (about 3 meters long) into the fork of a tree or onto two bi-pod support sticks at hip-height.
  3. Step 3: Ribbing Frame: Prop sturdy branches along both sides of the ridgepole at a 45-degree angle to create a tight wedge frame just large enough for your body.
  4. Step 4: Latching Lattice: Weave smaller leafy twigs horizontally across the ribbing to hold the insulation layer in place.
  5. Step 5: Thick Insulation Layer: Heap a thick layer (at least 60-90 cm / 2 to 3 feet thick) of dry leaves, pine needles, or ferns over the entire frame.
  6. Step 6: Ground Mattress: Fill the inside floor with at least 30 cm of dry leaves to prevent ground thermal transfer from draining body heat.
🏥4. Emergency First Aid & Trauma Response+

In grid-down situations, medical response times will be delayed. Immediate basic stabilizing interventions save lives.

Primary Assessment & Action Protocols:

  1. Step 1: Check Scene Safety: Ensure you are not putting yourself in danger before approaching an injured person.
  2. Step 2: Assess ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation): Open airway, verify breathing, check for severe bleeding.
  3. Step 3: Severe Hemorrhage Control:
    • Apply firm, direct pressure with sterile gauze or clean cloth directly onto the wound.
    • Wrap tightly with a compression bandage.
    • If life-threatening arterial bleeding on limbs continues, apply a commercial CAT tourniquet 5-7 cm above the wound site and tighten until bleeding stops. Note time of application on forehead.
  4. Step 4: Treating Shock: Lay patient flat on their back, elevate feet slightly (20 cm), cover with thermal space blanket to retain warmth, keep calm.
  5. Step 5: Burn Management: Flush burn immediately with cool running water for 10-20 minutes. Cover loosely with a clean, non-stick sterile dressing. Never break blisters or apply butter/grease.
  6. Step 6: Splinting Fractures: Immobilize the joint above and joint below the broken bone using rigid padded sticks and cloth ties. Check capillary refill in digits to ensure circulation is not cut off.
🧩5. Land Navigation, Compass & Mapping+

When GPS networks fail, magnetic compasses and topographic maps are essential for terrain navigation.

How to Take a Bearing Step-by-Step:

  1. Step 1: Orient the Map: Place compass flat on map. Rotate map until blue grid lines align with the magnetic needle pointing North.
  2. Step 2: Plot Destination: Lay the edge of your baseplate compass connecting your current location to your target destination on the map.
  3. Step 3: Dial the Bezel: Turn the rotating compass housing until the orienting lines match the N-S grid lines on the map.
  4. Step 4: Follow Bearing: Lift compass to chest level. Turn your body until the red magnetic needle rests inside the orienting arrow ("Put the Red in the Shed"). The direction-of-travel arrow points your exact heading.
  5. Step 5: Shadow-Stick Compass (Without Magnet): Push a 1-meter stick vertically into flat ground. Mark the tip of its shadow with a pebble. Wait 20 minutes and mark the new shadow tip. Draw a straight line between pebbles: the first mark is WEST, the second mark is EAST.
🪢6. Essential Knots & Lashings+

Knowing 4 key knots allows you to pitch tarps, secure loads, anchor guy lines, and build wilderness furniture.

1. Bowline (King of Knots)

Creates a fixed secure loop at the end of a rope that never slips under tension.

Step: Make a small loop (tree). Rabbit comes up out of hole, goes around tree, and back down into the hole. Tighten.

2. Taut-Line Hitch

Adjustable friction knot for tarp guy lines and tent stakes. Allows tightening saggy lines.

Step: Pass working end around anchor. Wrap twice inside loop towards anchor, then once outside loop. Pull tight.

3. Clove Hitch

Quick binding knot used to start lashings or tether a line to a post or tree trunk.

Step: Pass rope around post, cross over main line, wrap around post again, and tuck under the cross loop.

4. Square Lashing

Used to join two timber poles together at right angles (90°) for shelters and frames.

Step: Tie Clove Hitch to standing pole. Wrap tightly around both poles 3 times. Frapping wraps 2 times between poles. Finish with Clove hitch.

🧼7. Off-Grid Sanitation, Hygiene & Waste+

Poor sanitation leads to cholera, dysentery, and rapid disease outbreak during extended power or water grid failures.

Emergency Two-Bucket Toilet System:

  1. Step 1: Bucket Separation: Reserve Bucket #1 strictly for urine (liquid) and Bucket #2 lined with heavy-duty garbage bag for solid waste. Separating liquids drastically reduces odor and bacteria.
  2. Step 2: Carbon Cover Material: After each use of Bucket #2, cover waste with a 2 cm layer of dry sawdust, peat moss, crushed charcoal, or dried leaves.
  3. Step 3: Field Latrine (Outdoors): Dig a cathole 15-20 cm deep at least 60 meters away from water sources, camp sites, and trails. Cover thoroughly with soil after use.
  4. Step 4: Hand Wash Station Setup: Hang a water jug with a small nail hole at the bottom. Use biodegradable soap and scrub hands for 20 seconds before preparing food or treating injuries.
📻8. Emergency Radio Communications+

When cellular networks overload or go dark, handheld radios (PMR446, Baofeng, VHF) maintain team contact.

Comms Protocol Step-by-Step:

  1. Step 1: Pre-set Channels: Agree on primary channel (e.g. PMR Ch 3) and backup channel (PMR Ch 8) with team members prior to deployment.
  2. Step 2: 3x3x3 Rule: Turn on radio for 3 minutes at the top of every 3rd hour (e.g. 09:00, 12:00, 15:00, 18:00) to save battery while preserving contact windows.
  3. Step 3: Voice Transmission Method: Press PTT (Push-To-Talk), pause 1 second before speaking clearly. Keep transmissions short and concise:
    "[Recipient Call Sign], this is [Your Call Sign], Status Update, over."
  4. Step 4: Radio Terminology:
    • Over: Finished speaking, expecting response.
    • Out: Finished transmission, closing channel.
    • Wilco: Message received and will comply.
    • Say Again: Repeat last transmission.
🌱9. Emergency Food, Calorie Conservation & Foraging Safety+

Energy management is essential. Conserve movement and avoid poisonous plants in wild areas.

Universal Edibility Testing Protocol (Extreme Emergencies Only):

  1. Step 1: Universal Rules: Avoid plants with milky sap, umbrella-shaped flower clusters, bitter almond scent, fine hairs on stems, or red berries unless 100% positive on identification.
  2. Step 2: Skin Contact Test: Crush a small portion of the plant leaf. Rub on inside wrist or elbow. Wait 15 minutes to check for burning, itching, or redness.
  3. Step 3: Lip & Tongue Test: Touch a small piece to outer lip for 3 minutes. If no reaction, place on tip of tongue for 15 minutes. Do not swallow. If stinging occurs, spit out immediately and wash mouth.
  4. Step 4: Swallow Test: Swallow one tiny bite. Wait 8 hours without eating anything else. If no stomach cramps or nausea, plant is likely safe in small emergency quantities.
⚡10. Severe Weather & Natural Disaster Response+

Immediate automated reactions during natural hazards save crucial seconds.

🌍 Earthquake Protocol

DROP, COVER, HOLD ON: Drop to hands and knees. Cover head and neck under a sturdy table or desk. Hold on until shaking stops. If outdoors, move to open area away from power lines and buildings.

🌊 Flash Flood Protocol

SEEK HIGH GROUND: Never walk or drive through moving water. 15 cm (6 inches) of fast-flowing water can knock an adult down; 60 cm (2 feet) will float most motor vehicles.

🌪️ Severe Storm / High Wind Protocol

Move to the lowest level interior room (bathroom, hallway, cellar) away from exterior glass windows. Cover body with heavy blankets or mattress to protect against flying debris.

🏢11. Urban Evacuation & Bug-Out Protocols+

Evacuating populated areas during civil emergencies or severe weather mandates pre-planned routes and family coordination.

Step-by-Step Evacuation Plan:

  1. Step 1: Define PACE Communication Plan:
    • Primary: Mobile Phone Call/SMS
    • Alternate: Team Radio / PMR446
    • Contingency: Out-of-area relative message contact
    • Emergency: Physical pre-marked rally point on map
  2. Step 2: Route Mapping: Highlight 3 distinct evacuation routes out of your district (Primary arterial road, Secondary backroad, Tertiary walking route).
  3. Step 3: Vehicle Preparation: Keep vehicle fuel tank at least half full at all times. Maintain emergency car kit in trunk.
  4. Step 4: Bug Out Action: Grab 72-hour Bug Out Bags, shut off home main gas/water valves, secure property locks, and notify GET team of status before departing.
